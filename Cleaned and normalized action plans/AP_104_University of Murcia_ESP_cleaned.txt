coara-university of murcia action plan. doi: 10.5281/zenodo.14548768 university of murcia coara action plan (2023-2027) the university of murcia (umu) has demonstrated its institutional commitment to academic and research excellence standards by adhering in june 2020 to the human resources strategy for research staff (hrs4r). this step reflects our collective dedication to implementing innovative practices in human resources management within the field of research. this commitment was strengthened in november 2022 by signing the agreement on reforming research assessment and joining the coalition for advancing research assessment (coara). this institutional commitment consolidates umu's efforts to promote an evaluation that recognizes the diversity of contributions, introduces qualitative approaches, and appropriately uses traditional metrics as determining criteria. aligned with these principles, on 29th november 2024, the umu governing council unanimously approved the open, transparent, and merit-based recruitment policy (otm-r). this policy aims to promote the mobility of research staff and ensure equal opportunities and universal access, thereby fostering the exchange of knowledge and continuously enhancing the quality of teaching and research at the institution. this document outlines the umu coara action plan (2023-2027), designed to implement these principles in a structured and effective manner. the plan includes an introductory section contextualizing the national and international frameworks, offering accessible explanation of key concepts and tools. this approach is specifically tailored to help members of the university community new to the subject gain a basic understanding, while providing clarity and guidance for those already engaged in the research evaluation reform process. in recent years, umu has significantly progressed in developing regulations, procedures, and tools in order to ensure the application of the hrs4r principles and the reform of research assessment. these actions represent a fundamental pillar in the internal transformation of our institution, consolidating a model of research excellence aligned with european policies and the values of social responsibility and scientific integrity. index 1. introduction 2 2. international and national context 4 3. umu coara action plan (2023-2027) 5 4. timeline of the umu coara action plan (2023-2027) 13 5. bibliography 14 1 1. introduction the university of murcia (umu) is a public institution with a century-long history. it combines tradition and modernity in its commitment to academic excellence, innovative research, and social responsibility. with a community of more than 30,553 students, 2,643 teachers and researchers, and 1,214 technical and administrative staff, the umu stands as an educational and scientific pillar in the region of murcia. the university is distributed over five campuses strategically located in the metropolitan area of murcia and other regional cities: la merced, espinardo, el palmar, lorca, and san javier. each campus has distinctive characteristics that reflect the diversity and breadth of umu's academic and research offerings. each campus exhibits distinct characteristics, yet all share the common objective of addressing global societal demands and the specific needs of individual students. la merced campus is a historic urban site in the city center and the birthplace of the university houses hosting the faculties of arts, humanities, and law. in contrast, the espinardo campus constitutes the hub for most undergraduate and postgraduate programs, as well as a proactive center for different research initiatives. the health and biomedical campus in el palmar provides students direct interaction with patients and the broader community they serve. additionally, the sports sciences in san javier and the lorca campus further extend the university's presence across geographies of the region, each contributing to the institution's diverse educational landscape. umu is widely recognized for its inclusion and support services for people with disabilities. from 2022, it is a member of the european euniwell alliance (european university for well-being), founded in 2020 under the initiative of the european commission to promote partnerships between european universities. euniwell focuses on understanding, improving and balancing wellbeing at regional, european and global levels. this alliance consists of 11 leading universities, including cologne, florence, inalco, nantes, linnaeus, santiago de compostela, semmelweis, birmingham and the taras shevchenko national university of kiev. as a member of euniwell, umu brings its expertise to strategic areas such as veterinary medicine, agriculture, biomedicine, artificial intelligence, and teacher training. through this alliance, the university not only strengthens its internationalization in teaching and research but also offers unique opportunities for collaboration and mobility for students, faculty, research, and administrative staff, contributing to the wellbeing of local, regional, and global communities. since 2018, umu has been actively working on implementing the human resources strategy for researchers (hrs4r)1 aligning with the actions of the european research area (era)2. this institutional commitment underlines the promotion of good practices in academic and research staff recruitment as well as their working conditions and career paths. in 2022, this approach was reinforced with the signing of the agreement on reforming research assessment and the recent membership of the coalition for advancing research assessment (coara), marking a milestone in umu's commitment to adopt more inclusive evaluation systems oriented towards the quality and social impact of research. 2 by signing the agreement, umu committed itself to the following principles: core commitments 1. recognize the diversity of contributions and career paths in research, adapting to the needs and nature of each discipline. 2. prioritize qualitative assessment, where peer review is central, complemented by the responsible use of quantitative indicators. 3. avoid the inappropriate use of publication-based metrics, such as the journal impact factor and h-index, in research evaluation. 4. avoidance of rankings of research organizations as a criterion for scientific evaluation. supporting commitments 5. allocate the necessary resources to implement reforms in research evaluation processes. 6. in collaboration with stakeholders, review and develop evaluation criteria, tools, and processes to adapt them to different contexts and objectives. 7. raise awareness of evaluation reform by providing transparent communication, guidance and training on the new criteria and processes. 8. sharing practices and experiences to facilitate mutual learning within and outside the coalition, promoting coherence and avoiding fragmentation. 9. report on progress in implementing commitments, facilitating collective self- assessment and monitoring. 10. evaluate practices, criteria and tools based on sound evidence, ensuring that the data used is openly available to the research community. for these reasons, since 2023, the university of murcia has been actively pursuing the reform of research evaluation practices, integrating these changes across all processes and internal calls, with a particular emphasis on the assessment of individuals. 3 2. international and national contexts international context research evaluation reform is driven by international initiatives that seek the transformation of current systems into models that are more inclusive, fair, and aligned with the principles of scientific integrity and social impact. key references include: o the san francisco declaration on research assessment (dora, 2013)4 urges academic and funding institutions to adopt evaluation practices that focus on the intrinsic quality of research and eliminate reliance on metrics such as the journal impact index. o the leiden manifesto (2015)5 presents ten principles to promote the ethical, transparent, and contextual use of metrics, as well as to recognize the diversity of disciplinary approaches and encourage qualitative assessment complemented by accountable quantitative indicators. o the hong kong principles (2020)6 introduce an approach that prioritizes transparency, reproducibility and collaboration, emphasizing these criteria as fundamental to valuing researchers' contributions and their impact on society. at the european level, the european commission has led efforts to promote assessment systems aligned with these values. in 2022, the publication of the agreement on reforming research assessment was a milestone in setting out principles to overcome the limitations of traditional approaches. it led to the creation of the coalition for advancing research assessment (coara), an initiative designed to encourage evaluation practices that reflect scientific contributions and their relevance to society more fairly. coara's governance is structured inclusively to maximize the participation of its members, such as universities, funding agencies, and research centers. its main bodies include the general assembly, which acts as the highest decision-making body, responsible for defining strategic priorities and approving the coalition's main actions; the steering committee, which oversees the implementation of the agreement's principles and ensures its alignment with the founding objectives; and the secretariat, which provides administrative support and coordinates the coalition's operational activities. to address the different dimensions of reform, coara has established thematic working groups. these groups are made up of experts and stakeholders, and address specific issues such as accountable metrics, inclusive evaluation and transdisciplinarity. coara also encourages local implementation of its principles through the creation of national chapters, which adapt the global objectives to the particularities of each context, thus promoting the sustainability and relevance of reforms. 4 national context in spain, three national agencies lead the implementation of the reform: o national agency for quality assessment and accreditation (aneca): responsible for guaranteeing the quality of higher education in spain. its functions include evaluating and accreditation degrees, institutions, and teaching staff, ensuring national and international standards compliance. it also encourages continuous improvement through audits, certifications, and promoting good practices. aneca has adopted the principles of dora and coara7, leading the national chapter of the latter. o state research agency (aei): the main public body responsible for promoting scientific and technical research in all areas of knowledge. its functions include evaluating and granting public aid for research projects and human resources for research, promoting collaboration between scientific institutions, companies, and administrations, and promoting the internationalization of spanish science, aligning itself with the principles of dora. o instituto de salud carlos iii (isciii): specialized in biomedical research and public health, the isciii leads initiatives in translational research, epidemiological surveillance and researcher training. its functions include funding and evaluating research projects and human resources in biomedicine and health sciences, supporting clinical and translational research networks, training health professionals and researchers, and providing specialized scientific and technical services. it has also adhered to the principles of dora and coara. these agencies, along with other regional entities, have been instrumental in adapting evaluation procedures to align with the reform's objectives. their efforts have enabled universities to incorporate these frameworks into their internal systems for staff recruitment and research project assessment. in the region of murcia, the absence of a dedicated regional evaluation agency has led universities to depend primarily on national agencies. this reliance ensures that reforms implemented at the local level adhere to nationally and internationally recognized standards. 5 3. umu coara action plan (2023-2027) the umu coara action plan reflects the actions carried out in our university since 2023 to implement the agreement on reforming research assessment principles and the initiatives planned for 2025-2027. this plan is organized into three main blocks, aligned with the commitments adopted after signing the agreement. the first block includes actions to fulfill several supporting principles in terms of resource allocation, development of criteria and tools, training of the academic community, and exchange of good practices (commitments 5, 6, 7, and 8). the second block groups together the actions to comply with the core commitments and focuses on recognizing the diversity of research contributions, prioritizing the qualitative assessment, and avoiding the inappropriate use of traditional metrics (commitments 1, 2, 3, and 4). the third block corresponds to the actions designed to comply with evaluating and monitoring the reform implementation principles. it focuses on institutional self-evaluation and transparent communication of progress to the university community and the public (commitments 9 and 10). it is important to emphasize that this action plan is a dynamic process that will be modified, if necessary, as work progresses in the various international coara groups and our evaluation agencies. action line 1: constitution of the umu coara working group this strand focuses on fulfilling commitment 5 of the agreement to secure the necessary resources to implement the evaluation reforms at umu. specific actions include: • composition of the working group: this working group is initially made up of representatives from key areas of the institution, including vice-rectors with competences in research, teaching staff and knowledge transfer, as well as members of the umu research committee. this structure ensures representation from all areas of knowledge, promoting an inclusive approach to decision-making. this same working group unanimously agrees to involve as many university community members as possible. depending on the pilot projects to be carried out to implement the reform, specific working groups will be set up to allow the participation of representatives of all the groups involved in the adaptation of the new assessment model. • elaboration and approval of the umu coara action plan: this plan, whose implementation started in january 2023, has been consolidated and refined during the last months of 2024. 6 • specific budget allocation: an allocation of 5,000 euros has been approved within the umu’s general budget by the governing council on 18 december 2024 to finance coara-related actions. action line 2. communication, guidance, and training on the reform process this line responds to the commitment to raising awareness of the reform through communication and training strategies for the entire university community. actions include: • regular reporting to the governing council: regular updates on progress in implementing the reform agreement. • organization of informative seminars: spaces for dissemination aimed at explaining the proposed changes and their implications for teaching and research staff. • specialized training: sessions designed to familiarize participants with the new evaluation criteria and processes. action line 3. exchange of practices and experiences related to coara in order to facilitate mutual learning, this line fosters collaboration with national and international institutions and participation in coara forums. the main activities include: • collaboration with crue and the national chapter of coara: exchange of strategies and experiences between spanish universities. • participation in the coara general assemblies: these meetings, held every six months, allow umu to contribute to developing and refining reform policies. • international collaboration: participation in the coara working group to address specific challenges such as transdisciplinary evaluation and the social impact of research. 7 action line 4. compilation and study of the different international and national research evaluation systems this line addresses reviewing and developing evaluation criteria and tools aligned with coara principles. key activities include: • study of international evaluation systems: analysis of the best practices recommended by coara and their applicability in the umu context. • evaluation of national models: review of the tools used by aneca, miciun, aei and isciii to ensure coherence with national policies. action line 5. implementation of the reform of research assessment at umu this line directly addresses the key reform commitments, focusing on: 1. recognise the diversity of contributions and career paths in research, adapting to the needs and nature of each discipline. 2. prioritise qualitative assessment, where peer review is central, complemented by the responsible use of quantitative indicators. 3. avoid inappropriate use of publication-based metrics in research evaluation, such as journal impact factor and h-index. to this end, three pilot tests have been proposed covering different areas of university activity related to the evaluation of teaching and research staff at all stages of the academic career (from r1 to r4): • pilot project for the adaptation of calls for selecting human resources for research. it includes: - pre-doctoral contracts of the umu research promotion plan (r1). - pre-doctoral contracts associated with the knowledge generation call financed by the ministry of science, innovation and universities (miciu) (fpi-r1). - post-doctoral contracts associated with projects and research lines at umu (r2-r3). - pre-selection of applications in the beatriz galindo calls (r3-r4). the evaluation scales have been adapted taking as a reference the systems established by aneca, miciu, aei and isciii, guaranteeing coherence and validity in the processes: 8 aneca (national agency for quality assessment and accreditation): - evaluation of research activity. a procedure that recognizes the research work of university lecturers and research staff of public research bodies, awarding a remuneration supplement for each six-year period that has been positively evaluated. - academia program. the evaluation process allows university teaching staff to gain access to the teaching bodies of associate professor (r3) and full professor (r4), evaluating merits in teaching, research and leadership. - program for evaluating teaching staff for recruitment (pep) (r2-r3). assessment aimed at those who aspire to contract teaching posts in universities, such as permanent lecturer and private university lecturer. miciu (ministry of science, innovation and universities): - grants for university teacher training (fpu-r1). grants for research training in doctoral programs aim to facilitate the future incorporation of the beneficiaries into the spanish higher education and scientific research system. - beatriz galindo grants (r3-r4). a program that seeks to attract and retain research talent of excellence in spanish universities, promoting internationalization and improving the quality of the university system. aei (state research agency): - grants for contracts to train doctors in companies and other entities, known as industrial doctorates. with a duration of four years, these grants aim to promote industrial research or experimental development projects involving a doctoral thesis. - r3 certification (incentive program for talent attraction). recognition that certifies the research independence of scientists, facilitating their stable incorporation into the spanish science and technology system. - grants for talent attraction (atrae) (r4). this program seeks to attract excellent research talent to spanish universities, to promote the internationalization and quality of the university system. - ramón y cajal grants (r2-r3). grants aimed at promoting the incorporation of researchers with an outstanding track record in r&i centers, facilitating their employment stabilization. - juan de la cierva grants (r2). grants that promote the hiring of young phds by research organizations or r&i centers, encouraging their postdoctoral training. - abbreviated curriculum vitae (cva). the standard curriculum vitae format used in calls for research grants and projects, which includes an initial section where the researcher can highlight relevant aspects of his/her career (narrative cv). isciii (carlos iii health institute): - pre-doctoral health research training contracts (pfis): aid for the initial training of research personnel in health sciences and technologies through the completion of a doctoral thesis. - i-pfis contracts: iis-company phds in health sciences and technologies: aimed at training phds in environments that combine research in health research institutes (iis) and companies. 9 - río hortega contracts: these facilitate research preparation for health professionals who have completed their residency training. - juan rodés contracts: these facilitate the incorporation of doctors with research experience in the iis. - sara borrell and miguel servet grants. programs that finance the recruitment of researchers in the field of health sciences. the sara borrell grants aim for postdoctoral training (r2), while the miguel servet grants (r3) seek the incorporation of experienced researchers into the science and technology system. • pilot project for the adaptation of calls for the recruitment of teachers. a specific working group has been set up, including representatives from the teaching staff, research, studies, knowledge transfer, young researchers and trade unions. this group has worked on the modification of the scales for: - assistant professor phd. - associate professor. - substitute teacher. the reference models used to modify the scales are similar to those proposed in the previous section by the national agencies: aneca, miciu, aei and isciii. the proposed changes reflect a more inclusive and qualitative approach, aligning with coara principles. • pilot project for adapting the international doctoral school (eidum) calls. - extraordinary doctorate awards. the school's steering committee has led a project to update the criteria for awarding the best doctoral dissertations in the different programs, generating a single scale. the modifications consider the transition of researchers from r1 to r2 level and value early but very promising research career paths. the models proposed by aei and isciii in the bio-health field were taken as a reference. the proposed changes aim to reflect a more inclusive and qualitative approach aligned with coara principles. - incorporation of new phds into the doctorate programs. with the enactment of royal decree 576/2023, which regulates official doctoral studies, phd thesis supervisors within doctoral programs, as well as members of thesis evaluation committees, are now established to demonstrate a minimum of one six-year period of accredited research activity throughout their academic careers (as stipulated in articles 2.9 and 11.3). a merit recognition mechanism equivalent to the six-year research period will be proposed to promote the inclusion and active participation of early-career researchers (r2). the proposed mechanism draws upon the framework established by aneca for recognizing research activities. it adheres to the principles set forth by coara, ensuring consistency with best practices in academic assessment and inclusion. 10 action line 6. action on umu's ranking policy the university of murcia regularly contributes to sending the data requested by companies in international and national rankings, as until recently, this was a recommendation as part of the internationalization strategies of spanish universities. in the case of the umu, these data have been considered as one more indicator within the broad panel of indicators used by universities to measure the progress and fulfilment of its scientific policy objectives. for this reason, we are progressing towards the new model proposed by the reform to avoid the use of rankings as an evaluation criterion. the proposed actions include: • information and awareness-raising: disseminating how the assessment reform impacts umu's ranking policy. • annual review and monitoring: regular analysis of the implementation status of new policies, with progressive adjustments as necessary. action line 7. progress report on reform and its implementation this line reinforces umu's commitment with transparency and accountability. the measures include: • regular reports to the university community: systematic publication of progress and results on implementing the reform in the institution, encouraging participation and dialogue with all the groups involved. action line 8. evaluation of the implementation of the reform at umu continuous evaluation of the practices and tools implemented is key to ensuring their effectiveness and sustainability. activities include: • annual evaluation reports: analysis of the results obtained in the pilot projects, identify areas for improvement and propose adjustments. • refinement proposals: revising criteria and procedures based on the evidence gathered, ensuring the data used is accessible to the entire research community. 11 through these strategic actions, the umu coara action plan (2023–2027) aims to establish a comprehensive and sustainable framework for evaluating research and academic staff. this framework prioritizes inclusivity and equity, ensuring that diverse career paths and contributions are recognized. moreover, it aligns the evaluation process with international and national standards and principles. 4. umu coara action
