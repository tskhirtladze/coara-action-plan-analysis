eurashe – coara action plan background eurashe signed the agreement on reforming research assessment in january 2023, as part of its commitment to advancing the research landscape. as a member of the coalition for advancing research assessment (coara), eurashe is actively dedicated to participating in discussions on reforming the research assessment system and fully supports this important initiative. aligned with coara’s vision, eurashe endorses the recognition of diverse research outputs, practices, and activities in the evaluation of research, researchers, and research units. this commitment reflects eurashe’s understanding of the need for a more inclusive and comprehensive approach to research assessment. specifically, eurashe strongly supports the recognition of the diverse outputs of applied research. general reflections eurashe is a european association that represents applied universities1, and is not in itself a research-performing organisation (although the secretariat is involved in policy analysis). nonetheless, eurashe plays a crucial role in moving towards better forms of research assessment by raising awareness and contributing to the discussions on the societal, economic and regional implications of applied research. since there is no one-size-fits-all action plan for the diversity of its membership, the main role of eurashe is to guide the discussion and make observations or recommendations. this includes creating action plans at individual member institutions. given its mission, eurashe argues for recognizing the diversity of research outputs, particularly those arising from applied research and its contributions to innovation and research ecosystems. the influence of the applied sciences extends well beyond conventional scientific boundaries. often overlooked are the regional, economic and societal implications of applied research. eurashe actively participates in discussions concerning the diverse implications of applied research. eurashe recognises that a strict separation of basic research and applied research is artificial; both are interconnected. nevertheless, these two scientific domains have several fundamental differences. the main aim of basic or fundamental research is to enhance the existing body of knowledge, while applied sciences focus on developing practical solutions to societal challenges. given their distinct nature and objectives, different strategies are needed to evaluate the quality and impact of research in each domain. eurashe is firmly dedicated to reforming research assessment to ensure that various valuable contributions to science, particularly in applied sciences, are recognised accordingly. the following table shows eurashe’s guiding principles and position, as well as
